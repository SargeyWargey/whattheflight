iPhone

The nearest plane takes the center.

Flight number, airline, aircraft type, what it’s doing (climbing out, cruising, on approach), where it’s from and where it’s going, altitude, speed and how far away it is. Every other aircraft sits on the edge of the screen in its real compass direction, nose pointing the way it’s flying.

  • Turn and the sky turns with you. Icons slide around the edge, so whatever’s at the top is in front of you.
  • Swipe, tap, hold. Swipe for the next one out, tap any plane to bring it in, hold to pin it.
  • Your radius. Anything from 2 to 100 miles, in miles or kilometers.
  • Day and night. Paper and ink by day, a night radar after dark.

The full story, one tap away.

Tap the center for a photo of that exact aircraft, its route on a map, how far along the flight it is and when it will pass closest to you. If you’ve seen that same plane before, the app remembers.

Your favorites, in yellow.

Tap Favorite on any aircraft and it’s called out in yellow wherever it turns up: its icon on the radar and the map, a heart by its flight number, its name in your logbook. A favorite is the airframe itself, so it stays yellow whatever flight it’s on.

Pin up to five to keep them on the edge of your radar wherever they are in the world. For any that aren’t nearby, Where Is It Now? finds them. Favorites and pins follow you to iPad, Apple TV and Apple Watch.

Raise your iPhone to the sky.

Sky View opens the camera and labels the aircraft you can actually see, right where they are. Line one up to lock on, zoom in and take a photo.

Every photo is filed in your logbook, under Photos and on that aircraft’s own page, as a boarding pass with the flight, the aircraft and the moment you spotted it, ready to share.

Know before it’s overhead.

Get a heads-up a couple of minutes before an aircraft passes right over you, and whenever something unusual is around: an emergency squawk, a military or government flight, an air ambulance or a rare aircraft.

Heading out? Turn on Sky Watch and the app keeps watching with your iPhone in your pocket, for up to two hours. The next aircraft heading your way counts down on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.

  • Your idea of overhead. Choose how close an aircraft has to come before it counts.
  • On your wrist. Alerts reach Apple Watch, where Find in the Sky points you straight at the aircraft.
  • On the big screen. Apple TV shows them on the radar and can read them aloud.

Every plane that passes, logged.

Every aircraft that comes inside your radius goes in your logbook: when and where you saw it, how close it came, and whether it was a first. Dig into the numbers for each place you watch from, chase records and earn 46 badges, from First Contact to Queen of the Skies. It all syncs across iPhone, iPad and Apple TV with iCloud.

Today’s highlights beside your totals, the badges you’re closest to, the last two weeks, your records and the planes you see most.

Make it yours.

Pick the map behind your radar and the icon on your Home Screen, keep the nearest aircraft on a widget, or just ask Siri.

Four maps, or plain paper

Contour lines drift slowly behind the radar, like the tide, at the speed you choose. This page is drawn in Survey. Pick yours in Settings → Display → Topography.

  • OffPlain paper
  • RemasterBig gentle hills, one line weight
  • SurveyA survey map, every fifth line heavier
  • HighlandsRidges and winding valleys, the most detail
  • ReliefSurvey lines over faint hill shading

Widgets and Siri

The Overhead widget keeps the nearest aircraft on your Home Screen, with what else is up there, and there are smaller ones for the Lock Screen. Or don’t look at all: ask Siri.

iPad

Room for the whole sky.

On iPad the radar fills the screen, by day or by night. Turn it sideways with Map view on and a live map of the traffic sits beside the radar, turning as you turn.

On a folding iPhone, open it halfway: radar on one side of the fold, map on the other.

iPhone Duo

Half radar. Half map.

Fold it shut and the radar keeps going on the cover screen. Open it halfway and it splits at the fold: the radar on one side, a live map of the same planes on the other. Open it flat for one big radar, or stand it on a table and let it keep watch.

Apple Watch

Point at a plane.

A radar on your wrist with its own GPS. Raise your arm toward an aircraft and the Watch tells you what it is, or pick one and let haptics lead you to it. Complications show what’s nearest and count down to the next one overhead.

Apple TV

The sky on the big screen.

A radar, a map, or both side by side, with alerts read aloud when something flies over. Send your spot and the way your TV faces from your iPhone in one tap.

No account. No ads.

There’s nothing to sign up for and no tracking. Your logbook stays on your devices and in your own iCloud.
