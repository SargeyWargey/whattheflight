Your sky, not your data

Privacy policy, effective October 26, 2026

The short version: What The Flight? has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. I (the developer) never receive your location or anything else about you. To show the aircraft around you, the app asks a flight-data service for the traffic in an area around your position. Everything else stays on your devices or in your own iCloud.

Who this covers

This policy covers the What The Flight? apps for iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Apple TV, made by Josh Sargent ("I", "me"). Questions go to [your support email].

Your location

  • The app uses your location, with your permission, to find the aircraft near you. It asks for access only while you’re using it.
  • To get live traffic, the app sends Flightradar24 the boundaries of an area around your position (the size of the radius you choose). Your position can be worked out from that area. The request doesn’t include your name, an account or any device identifier, but like any internet request it comes from your IP address. Flightradar24 handles requests under its own privacy policy.
  • When you turn on Sky Watch, the app keeps using your location in the background for up to two hours so it can alert you to aircraft passing overhead. iOS shows the blue location indicator while it runs, and you can stop it at any time.
  • The Apple TV app has no GPS. It uses a location you type in, search for, send from your iPhone, or the Apple TV’s own approximate location.
  • Place names in your logbook come from Apple’s geocoding service, and maps come from Apple Maps, both under Apple’s privacy policy.

Searching for an aircraft

When you search for a flight, pin an aircraft that’s far away, or ask Where Is It Now?, the app asks Flightradar24 for that flight number, callsign or tail number, wherever it is in the world. Nothing about you is sent beyond the IP address every internet request has.

Aircraft photos

Aircraft photos come from Planespotters.net. To find one, the app sends the aircraft’s registration or transponder code. Nothing about you is sent beyond the IP address every internet request has.

Your logbook and Sky View photos

  • Your logbook (the aircraft you’ve seen, where and when) and the photos you take in Sky View are stored on your device.
  • If you turn on Sync with iCloud, they’re also stored in your private iCloud account so your iPhone, iPad and Apple TV share one logbook. Only you can read your private iCloud data. I can’t see it.
  • Your pinned and favorite aircraft are kept in your private iCloud too, so they follow you to your iPad, Apple TV and Apple Watch, whether or not the logbook syncs. They’re a list of tail numbers and flight numbers, nothing about where you were.
  • When you save a Sky View photo to your photo library, it includes the flight details and the location where you took it, like any camera photo. The app can only add photos to your library. It can’t read them.

Camera, motion and local network

  • Camera: Sky View shows the camera image with labels on top. The image is processed on your device to find aircraft and is never uploaded.
  • Motion: on iPhone, Sky View and lifting the phone to open it use the motion sensors to tell where the camera points, and linking an Apple TV uses them to tell which way the TV faces. On Apple Watch, Identify and Find read them to tell which way your arm points. Motion readings are used on the device and never stored or sent.
  • Local network: linking your iPhone to your Apple TV sends your location and the direction your TV faces directly to the Apple TV on your own network. It doesn’t go over the internet.

Notifications, widgets and Siri

Alerts are created on your device, and spoken alerts are read by the device’s own voice. Widgets, Apple Watch complications, Siri and the Apple TV Top Shelf ask Flightradar24 for the traffic around the spot the app last watched, the same way the app does. The app shares that spot with them on your device. The Live Activity shows what the app already has.

What I don’t do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No advertising, analytics or third-party tracking code.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.
  • No collection of data from children. The app is rated 4+ and doesn’t ask anyone for personal information.

Your choices

  • Turn location access off at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services. Demo traffic still works without it.
  • Turn off Keep a logbook or Sync with iCloud in the app’s Settings → Logbook, and delete sightings or photos from the logbook.
  • Deleting the app deletes everything it stored on that device. Data in your iCloud can be removed in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Storage.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
